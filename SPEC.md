## PRODUCT PURPOSE

A proprietary AI-powered world-building tool for print-on-demand sellers.

Sold on the back of a paid world-building challenge. May ultimately live
alongside the Listing Factory as a subscription product. **It is not a product
that connects to the challenge — it is a tool used inside the challenge, sold
separately beyond it.**

The challenge teaches sellers how to build a customer world. The software makes
that process dramatically easier to execute and maintain.

The software should help sellers:

- build around a customer world instead of constantly jumping between unrelated niches
- become increasingly fluent in one customer
- stay immersed in that customer's culture
- develop weekly product drops inside the world
- visually build and track those drops
- converse with a research-informed simulation of the customer
- maintain a long-term creative history of the world
- eventually use mature Etsy performance data to deepen what is working

Visually exciting and creatively useful. **Not** a chatbot wrapped in a
dashboard. **Not** giant research reports, maps, categories, customer dossiers,
or unnecessary information.

> The AI can process a large amount of information behind the scenes.
> The user experience should stay simple.

---

## CORE PHILOSOPHY

**WORLD BUILDING MAKES THE SELLER FLUENT.**

A seller becomes better at emotionally connecting with a customer when they stay
inside one customer world long enough to understand that customer deeply.

The seller should still test aggressively. Everything remains rooted in data.
The difference is that the seller tests multiple data-backed sub-niches **inside
the same broader customer world** instead of constantly jumping between
unrelated customer groups. This creates compounding customer knowledge.

The tool must support this philosophy rather than replace the seller's judgment.

---

## CRITICAL RULE: WORLDS ARE DISCOVERED FROM THE BOTTOM UP

The seller does **not** start by saying "I want to build a faith-based shop" or
"I want to build a horse shop." That is backwards.

The seller begins with eRank research. They find individual sub-niche keywords
that show legitimate demand and acceptable competition. For example: *Jesus loves
you shirt · Psalms-related products · Christian motherhood phrases · specific
faith identity keywords · specific Scripture-related searches.*

Only after finding multiple viable sub-niches does the seller recognise: these
demand pockets are all serving the same broader customer world.

**The broader world is discovered through the sub-niches. The software must
reflect this.**

---

## THE WORLD FRAMEWORK

### W — WORK UP FROM DEMAND

The seller researches inside eRank independently. The AI does not claim to know
whether an Etsy keyword has good demand or low competition. The seller brings
the actual researched keywords into the tool.

**Minimum viable world = at least 6 independently validated sub-niche keywords
that clearly point toward the same broader customer universe.**

Six is the minimum. Ideally more. The point is to prevent users choosing a world
because they simply like the idea of it. The world has to emerge from actual
marketplace opportunities.

Prompt: *Add at least 6 sub-niche keywords you validated in eRank.* Each entered
individually. The user can always return later and add more — this is **not** a
one-time locked onboarding screen. The keyword collection is a permanent part of
the world profile.

### O — OWN THE WORLD

Demand alone is not enough. The seller also needs natural interest, emotional
connection, curiosity, or appreciation for the customer. Liking the customer
world is an early indicator that fluency will be easier to build. A seller who
actively dislikes or cannot relate to the customer is choosing a harder road.

Several simple questions. **Not** a psychological assessment. 1–10 scales:

1. How much does this world genuinely interest you?
2. How likely would you be to personally wear, buy, display, or gift something from this world?
3. How interested are you in spending months learning more about this customer?
4. When you see products in this niche, do you naturally understand why someone would want them?

The purpose is reflection. **The AI must not issue a verdict that the world is
approved or rejected.** The seller makes that judgment.

### R — RESEARCH UNTIL YOU SPEAK IT

The seller needs to build a mental archive of the world. This is where **World
Daily** becomes a major part of the software.

During initial setup the seller also uploads **visual calibration**: about 6
existing designs in this customer world whose creative style they genuinely love
and could imagine designing alongside.

These are **not** proof of fluency. **Not** demand evidence. **Not** products
the AI should copy. Their purpose is to help the AI understand the visual
direction the seller is imagining. Replaceable and updatable later.

### L — LAYER THE WORLD

The customer world will grow. The seller may discover additional sub-niches,
adjacent lifestyle interests, new aesthetics, unexpected product categories,
everyday styles, seasonal behaviors, cultural overlaps.

**The tool must never police this expansion.** The AI must not say "this does
not belong in your world," "this is 82% aligned," "this product is off-brand,"
"reject this idea," "this does not fit your customer."

AI is too unreliable at those judgments. The seller is the authority.

> A rave and festival shop might discover its customers also buy everyday men's
> streetwear with rose graphics. A simplistic AI could incorrectly decide rose
> streetwear does not belong in a rave world. The seller may understand the same
> customer wears this style outside the event environment.

The AI helps explore possibilities, never hands down brand judgments. The seller
can continually add new layers as understanding develops.

### D — DROP. DATA. DEEPEN.

Use this exact phrase.

The seller builds products in structured drops. Recommended rhythm: **10 designs
every week, published Friday.** The software tracks the current drop
automatically from the calendar. **The seller can pause the schedule at any
time.**

The tool must not assume the previous week's Etsy performance should guide the
next week's drop. Etsy often needs far longer than seven days to produce useful
listing data.

Workflow: create drops consistently → allow older drops to gather meaningful
data → review those drops when sufficient time has passed → deepen based on
mature evidence. The current drop continues being created while earlier drops
are still gathering data.

## PRIMARY PRODUCT EXPERIENCE

A very small number of strong experiences. **Do not create a sprawling dashboard
with fifteen tools.**

1. World Profile / editable setup
2. World Daily
3. Drop Studio
4. Talk to the Customer
5. Drop History / mature data review

---

## 1. WORLD PROFILE

Foundational information established through WORLD. **Not** a one-time
onboarding wizard that disappears. Always revisitable and modifiable. Think of
setup as several editable sections or buttons.

**Validated Sub-Niches** — user-entered eRank keywords, minimum six to establish
the initial world. The system does not validate demand itself; the seller is
confirming these have already been researched. They can continually add more.

**Personal Connection** — the affinity questions from OWN THE WORLD. Revisitable.

**Visual Calibration** — approximately six uploaded design references. They tell
the AI what creative style the seller is envisioning. **They should appear
visually rather than as file names.** Removable, replaceable, extendable.

**Active World Areas** — specific areas of the customer's world the seller wants
continuously watched. These power World Daily. **The seller chooses these; the
AI does not decide what matters.** Example for rave/festival: festival fashion,
EDM culture, festival events, streetwear, nightlife, rave humor, festival
beauty. Editable at any time.

---

## 2. WORLD DAILY

A small personalised newspaper about the seller's customer world. One of the
central reasons someone would keep paying.

Goal: continuous immersion. World building requires fluency. Fluency requires
exposure. The seller should not spend hours daily searching the internet to stay
close to the culture.

The AI researches the active world areas behind the scenes, then gives a very
small number of genuinely useful daily updates. **Do not create an endless feed.
Do not dump the research. Do not show twenty-five updates because the AI found
twenty-five things.** Target ≈ **5 meaningful daily items.**

Possible items: a phrase appearing repeatedly · a meme gaining traction · a
visual styling pattern · an event approaching · a cultural conversation · a
product or object appearing frequently · a micro-aesthetic gaining visibility ·
a recurring joke · a lifestyle behavior · a relevant celebrity or creator moment
· a seasonal ritual beginning · an adjacent interest becoming visible.

Show the signal and enough context to understand why it is interesting. **Do not
say "you should make a shirt about this."** Customer immersion, not automated
product directives. The seller decides what is useful.

Desired feeling: *"I can open this every morning for five minutes and understand
what is happening inside my customer's universe."* Not: *"Here is another
research report I need to study."*

### Screen

Header: `GOOD MORNING / HERE'S WHAT'S HAPPENING IN YOUR WORLD TODAY`

Then approximately five editorial update cards, easy to scan. Closer to a
stylish digital publication than a SaaS analytics dashboard. Avoid dense tables,
twelve graphs, giant blocks of AI text.

## 3. DROP STUDIO

The central creative workspace. Must be visually strong — like standing in front
of a design wall building the next release.

**Desktop layout: ~70% left Drop Board / ~30% right AI creative conversation.**

### Drop Board

A large horizontal workspace that loosely simulates looking at products together
inside an Etsy shop. **It is not pretending to predict Etsy performance.** It is
a creative visualization workspace.

Top: the seller's shop banner, visible across every drop. Below it:

```
DROP 06
SEPTEMBER 25
7 / 10 DESIGNS
```

Then a grid of ten Etsy-style listing tiles. The seller uploads mockups into
those tiles as each design is created. Tiles should resemble Etsy listing
thumbnails enough to give the feeling of seeing the collection together in a
storefront. **Do not clone Etsy exactly.**

**Empty slots remain visible** — 7 mockups, 3 blank tiles — so the seller
immediately understands what remains.

**Board background** is customizable: white, ivory, pale pink, other simple
custom color. Different mockups read differently against different backgrounds.
Keep simple — **do not turn it into a full design editor.**

### Drop AI / Creative Conversation

Contextual to the current drop. Not a disconnected general chatbot.

The AI knows: validated sub-niches · current world profile · active world areas
· visual calibration references · World Daily history · previous drops · current
uploaded mockups · research the seller has added · current drop date and
progress.

The seller talks naturally while looking at the drop: *"I'm stuck on the last
three." · "These are starting to feel repetitive." · "I want to push this
collection further." · "I want one design that feels completely unexpected." ·
"Help me think about this keyword differently." · "I want to add hats to this
drop." · "Give me a few different directions I could explore for the remaining
designs."*

**The AI helps the seller think. The AI does not approve or reject the seller's
creative judgment.**

The AI should automatically know which drop is open and which mockups belong to
it. The user should never have to say "these are my seven designs."

### Scheduling

Default 10 designs per week, drop day Friday, tracked automatically. When a new
weekly cycle begins the software creates the next drop.

**Pause control is required.** People travel, get sick, publish on different
schedules. Pausing stops automatic progression until resumed.

### Completing a drop

At the publishing point the drop becomes a completed visual artifact. **Do not
delete or replace it.** DROP 06 freezes into Drop History; the workspace becomes
`DROP 07 · 0 / 10`.

This creates a long-term visual record. After months the seller scrolls Drop 01
→ 06 and sees how the world developed. **This archive itself is valuable.**

### Drop status

`Building` · `Live` · `Gathering Data` · `Ready to Review`

**Do not create automatic AI judgments about winners or losers.** The statuses
simply organise lifecycle.

## 4. TALK TO THE CUSTOMER

A conversation with a research-informed simulation of the customer inside the
world. Not a replacement for real research — a way to become more fluent and
think from inside the customer's life.

Informed by: world profile · active world areas · accumulated world research ·
World Daily research · customer-language patterns the system has learned · the
seller's stored context · relevant current cultural context.

Normal human questions: *"What are you doing this weekend?" · "What are you
obsessed with right now?" · "Where do you shop?" · "What stores do you always
check first?" · "What are you wearing lately?" · "What are you sick of seeing?"
· "What do you and your friends send each other?" · "What do you spend way too
much money on?" · "What are you saving on Pinterest lately?" · "What do you buy
before an event?" · "What are you excited about next month?" · "What would make
you immediately want a sweatshirt?"*

Answers can spark new research directions, collection concepts, product ideas,
cultural questions, lifestyle understanding, deeper fluency.

It must feel like talking to a believable member of the customer world, **not** a
generic avatar like *"Ashley, 27, lives in Austin, drinks lattes."* The
simulation represents the broader researched customer world. **It is a tool for
exploration. It is not an oracle.**

When appropriate it can reference things currently happening in the world — if a
relevant event is approaching, *"What are you doing this weekend?"* could
surface it, and the seller realises people are preparing for that right now.

### Screen

```
TALK TO YOUR CUSTOMER
WORLD: FESTIVAL + RAVE
```

Then the conversation. Optional starting prompts, not rigid categories.

---

## 5. DROP HISTORY

Completed drops displayed visually, scannable chronologically:

```
DROP 01 — LIVE              thumbnail grid preview
DROP 02 — READY TO REVIEW   thumbnail grid preview
DROP 03 — GATHERING DATA    thumbnail grid preview
```

Clicking a drop opens the full frozen Drop Board. **A creative archive, not
accounting software.**

### Data + Deepen

Recent Etsy data must not influence the next weekly drop automatically. Older
drops eventually become ready for review. **Do not create fake certainty.** Do
not say "the market has proven X" unless the data supports it. The important
behavior is maintaining history so mature data can be connected back to the
exact creative drop that produced it.

---

## RELATIONSHIPS

**World Daily ↔ Talk to the Customer** — Daily: *"here are the things happening
around this customer."* Talk: *"let me explore what those things might mean from
inside the customer's life."*

**World Daily → Drop Studio** — World Daily is **not** an automatic idea
generator. The seller may use something or completely ignore it. **The system
preserves the seller as creative director.**

**Drop Studio → Listing Factory** — Do not send incomplete ideas into Listing
Factory. It cannot create a listing until the seller has actually created the
design and has the product assets. Correct order: understand the world → develop
and organise the ten-design release → **the seller actually creates the
designs** → upload completed mockups → then products move through Listing
Factory. **Do not skip the design-creation step.**

**Goldie** — the existing Design GPT. Do not recreate it here. Do not turn this
into a design generator. Goldie stays separate. This product's job is world
fluency + ongoing research + drop development + creative organization. Avoid
feature overlap.

---

## WHAT THE AI MUST NOT DO

Critical. Do not build any of these.

- **No fake eRank intelligence.** The AI cannot know whether a keyword is
  genuinely low competition without a legitimate data source. The seller does
  the eRank research. **Never fabricate marketplace validation.**
- **No world-fit scoring.** No "82% on-world," "91% aligned," "this fits your
  world," "this does not fit," "this violates your brand," "off-world." **Do not
  create a brand police system.**
- **No approval / rejection system.** No approve, reject, canon, not canon,
  green light, red light. The software explores implications. It does not decide
  what the seller is allowed to build.
- **No giant customer intelligence map.** No rituals / visual codes / identity
  clusters / emotional territories / fifteen expandable nodes / endless
  taxonomy. Overwhelming. The AI processes complexity behind the scenes; the
  seller experiences clarity.
- **No giant research dashboard.** Do not make the user read everything the AI
  discovers. World Daily distills it.
- **No cheesy mood-board generator as the main product.** Too shallow, easily
  reproduced elsewhere.
- **No Etsy shop simulator as a standalone gimmick.** The Etsy-like presentation
  belongs only inside Drop Studio because it helps see ten products together.
- **No premature weekly data interpretation.** Friday's new listings are not
  evidence for the following Friday's drop.
- **No autonomous creative director.** The AI may expand, question, suggest,
  research, simulate, surface, contextualize, organize. It must not dictate,
  grade, police, approve, reject, or pretend certainty.

## INITIAL USER FLOW

1. User creates a new world.
2. Enters at least six eRank-validated sub-niche keywords. The system makes
   clear: *these should already have been validated by you inside eRank.*
3. Answers the personal-interest / affinity questions.
4. Uploads approximately six visual design references they love.
5. Identifies the active parts of the customer world to continuously research.
6. The system creates their World Profile.
7. They land inside World Daily.
8. Their first Drop Studio is created: `DROP 01 · FRIDAY, AUGUST 28 · 0 / 10 DESIGNS`.

---

## WEEKLY LOOP

**During the week** — check World Daily · do own eRank research · keep learning
the customer · use Talk to the Customer when useful · develop designs · upload
completed mockups into Drop Studio · use the Drop AI when help is wanted.

**Friday** — ten-design drop published · drop becomes Live · next drop created.

**Following weeks** — past drops gather data · seller keeps creating · older
drops become Ready to Review · seller uses mature information to deepen.

---

## SUBSCRIPTION VALUE

The product must remain useful after the world is initially built.

Continuous **immersion** (World Daily) · continuous **interaction** (Talk to the
Customer) · continuous **creation** (Drop Studio) · continuous **memory** (Drop
History) · continuous **deepening** (mature data).

The product becomes more valuable the longer the seller uses it.

## HOW THIS CONNECTS TO THE CHALLENGE

The challenge teaches the WORLD framework. The software is the surprise reveal
that makes the framework dramatically easier to execute.

> "Now I understand how to build a world."
> "Wait. You built software that helps me actually live inside this process every week?"

The challenge provides the thinking, methodology, customer-world philosophy,
eRank research process, discipline, creative education.

The software provides structure, immersion, memory, research assistance,
customer simulation, weekly execution environment.

**The software should never replace the thinking taught in the challenge. It
operationalizes it.**

## BUILD PRIORITY

Build the smallest version that fully expresses the idea.

| Phase | Scope |
|---|---|
| **1** | World Profile — validated sub-niche input, affinity questions, visual calibration uploads, active world areas |
| **2** | Drop Studio — shop banner, 10 mockup slots, drop number/date, custom board background, weekly schedule, pause, right-side contextual AI |
| **3** | World Daily — automated research against chosen active areas, ~5 distilled daily updates |
| **4** | Talk to the Customer — research-informed simulation, world context, current cultural context |
| **5** | Drop History — freeze completed drops, chronological archive, drop statuses |
| **6** | Data + Deepen — attach mature Etsy performance to older drops, allow revisiting after enough data exists |

**Do not let Phase 6 distort the initial product.** The initial magic is:
understand the world · stay inside the world · build the next drop.

---

## FINAL PRODUCT TEST

Before adding any feature, ask:

1. Does this help the seller become more fluent in the customer?
2. Does this make building weekly drops inside that world easier?

**If the answer to both is no, do not add it.**
